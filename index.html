Client-side building simulation

EnergyPlus in the browser

The U.S. Department of Energy whole-building engine, compiled to WebAssembly and running inside this tab. It solves a single free-running zone against any of 17,292 weather stations and the design conditions that come with them. The sheet opens on Denver's design days, so there is a result on the page before you have chosen anything. The model, the solver and the results never leave your machine — there is no simulation server behind this page.

      Where it goes

      The targets

      What each standard asks of the finished building, against what the last run read. The specifications that would get you there are on the console, folded beside the controls they set; this board only keeps the score.

      Chase a standard and it collapses to its single worst line, held up beside the drawing while you work, with a ghost of where that margin stood when you took hold — so you can see whether the control under your hand is helping without reading a dozen rows a screen away. Chasing measures; it decides nothing, and pressing it again puts it down.

      Kept schemes

      A kept scheme is the whole desk, stored as the same link Copy scheme link hands out — one format for keeping and for sharing, carried forward by the same version ledger, so a scheme kept today still opens on a page that has since grown a channel. It is held in this browser and goes nowhere else. An option can be put down, another one worked, and the two set side by side.

      Nothing kept yet. Save this scheme, in the run log at the left, keeps the desk as it stands together with whatever it was reading.

      Model console
      
        
          
          
          
          
        
        
      
          
      
            Eighteen channels in the order the physics happens. Every control writes a real object
            into the IDF, and every strip reads back what its path is actually contributing. The five
            marked rail are terms of the zone air heat balance, and they sum at the foot:
            positive is heat arriving in the zone air, negative is heat leaving it.
          
      
          
          
          
      
            Build it to
            
      
            
      
              A standard writes only what it has an opinion about; the rest of the desk stays
              yours. Conformance is measured off the controls, never remembered.
